Ryūtai: dragon writing and present-day interpretive practice

What contemporary sources claim

Tokuma Shoten’s description of Mori Michiyo’s 2024 book presents a 48-character system with spiritual powers and a Futomani arrangement. It assigns particular purposes to signs and pairs: for example, tsuru for gathering people, muku for spreading something, ku for financial fortune, and kini for health. These are the publication’s attributed interpretations, not meanings supplied by the chart labels below. Publisher-supplied book description.

A 2022 announcement from the Dragon Letter promotion organization describes workshops developed under Mori’s guidance, using 48 sounds and sixteen paired combinations. It presents automatic writing as part of her involvement. This identifies a contemporary teaching and dissemination network. Organization’s announcement.

A divine creator and a remote origin

The book description attributes Ryūtai to the deity Umashiashikabihikoji approximately 5,500 years ago and portrays it as a secret of the imperial household and a gift from a higher-dimensional world. Its Futomani discussion associates the arrangement with divine beings. These elements explain how the writing is situated within a sacred narrative. Source: book description and contents.

The promotion organization likewise identifies Umashiashikabihikoji as the creator. The presence of that deity’s name in a modern announcement documents the organization’s account; a separate question is where an early written witness for the attributed script can be examined. Source: organization’s statement.

Documentation and provenance — a separate record

Two inventories. The reference collection’s Tatsu / Ryūtai chart supplies the images below. It has 51 labeled positions: a full five-by-ten grid plus N. The contemporary book describes 48 characters. This website preserves the 51-position reference as a named chart variant; it does not claim it is Mori’s exact 48-sign set.

What has been consulted. The publisher’s public description and contents establish the book’s advertised claims and publication details. The full book has not been transcribed here. The organization’s public announcement establishes its own teaching claims. The image collection supplies a reading key, not a test of claimed effects or a dated ancient manuscript.

Further research. Compare the 48-sign teaching chart directly against this 51-position variant. Record exactly which forms differ or are omitted before transferring any sign-specific interpretation. For historical origin, locate the earliest manuscript and custody record; for claimed effects, distinguish a reported experience from a documented procedure and independent observations.

Reviewed 18 October 2026. Attributed spiritual and health claims are recorded as claims, separately from the source record.
