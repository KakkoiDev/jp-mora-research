Ahiru / Abiru: signs attributed to a transmitted tradition

What the chart records

The linked Jindai Moji Collection labels these signs Ahiru and also uses the name Mononobe. It supplies two 47-sign chart variants, with historical WI and WE and gaps at YI, YE, and WU. The first is displayed below; the second can be opened for comparison. The Latin labels belong to the source chart. Source chart.

Both variants belong to this catalog’s Ahiru entry. They should not be silently equated with every other chart called Mononobe, nor with the separately listed Ahiru cursive set. Names can travel between collections while the drawn forms differ.

Legends and transmission stories

A transmission account associates Ahiru writing with the Urabe and Abiru families of Tsushima. The family’s documents are said to preserve it alongside other character traditions. Comparative survey, Ahiru section.

The broader divine-writing narrative describes an original Japanese writing preserved through sacred lineages after Chinese writing became dominant. Kokugakuin’s historical account records Hirata Atsutane’s 1819 collection and his acceptance of two Hifumi forms, true and cursive, as divine writing. That story of preservation explains the authority claimed for the signs; it should remain distinguishable from the date of a particular copy. Mori Mizue, Encyclopedia of Shinto.

Documentation and provenance — a separate record

Graphical witness. The images below are the online collection’s small reference graphics, loaded from its publisher. They are not photographs of an ancient object. Each table links back to the exact chart. Their 47-sign inventory is retained rather than completed with invented forms.

Historical discussion. Kokugakuin also records arguments against divine-writing theories, including the relationship discussed between Hifumi and Hangul. This is part of the history of interpretation and debate. It is separate from the legend above and from a direct examination of a specific family document. Historical overview.

Further research. Locate a dated witness for each chart variant; record its owner, title, copyist, and reading key. Compare signs and their organization individually. A resemblance, a family attribution, and an uninterrupted chain of custody are three different kinds of information.
