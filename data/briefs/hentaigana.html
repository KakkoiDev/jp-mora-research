Many shapes for the same sound

What the sources describe

Hentaigana are historical alternatives within the hiragana tradition. A reading can have several forms because writers used different parent kanji, or developed different cursive shapes from the same kanji. They are useful for reading handwritten books, signs, names, and calligraphy. A catalog of forms therefore has more entries than a chart of sounds. Unicode’s account of historical kana.

The National Institute for Japanese Language and Linguistics (NINJAL) catalogs each form by a research identifier, reading, parent character, and, where available, Unicode and MJ identifiers. Its guide also identifies reference dictionaries and historical type specimens used for comparison. A research identifier describes a catalog entry; a Unicode number identifies an encoded character. They are related records, not interchangeable numbering systems. NINJAL catalog guide.

Legends and cultural associations

As part of the hiragana tradition, these forms share the story that Kūkai, or Kōbō Daishi, created kana and the Iroha poem. That is a story about the script’s origin and authority, rather than a separate creator legend for each variant. See the kana tab’s legend section for the account and its dating discussion. This catalog does not assign a deity or spiritual power to each hentaigana.

Documentation and provenance — a separate record

The reference below combines all 293 entries in the NINJAL list with the encoded forms absent from that particular list. The result contains 286 encoded forms, counting archaic YE as E-1, and 28 additional catalog forms with no code point listed there. Each entry links to its institute record or the relevant Unicode chart. “No assignment listed” reports this source’s field; it is not a claim that the form can never be encoded.

Reading the evidence. A parent kanji identifies a graphical derivation; it does not automatically supply the meaning of a word written with that kana. When researching a specimen, retain the manuscript, date, reading, and exact shape together. Multiple readings occur, so some forms will not fit a single sound cell.
